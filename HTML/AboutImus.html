City Profile



Imus, officially known as the City of Imus, is a first-class component city in the province of Cavite, Philippines. Renowned as the "Flag Capital of the Philippines," it holds a significant place in history as the site of pivotal battles during the Philippine Revolution against Spanish colonization.

Today, Imus thrives as a dynamic urban hub, blending residential, commercial, and industrial developments. The city is home to iconic landmarks such as the Imus Cathedral, the Imus Heritage Park, and the Battle of Alapan Monument. Its vibrant culture is showcased through festivals like the Wagayway Festival, which honors its rich historical legacy.

Strategically located near Metro Manila, Imus serves as a vital gateway to the southern provinces of Luzon. The city continues to progress, fostering opportunities in business, education, and tourism while steadfastly preserving its historical and cultural heritage.

History

Imus, once a "visita" of Cavite Viejo (now Kawit), is among Cavite's oldest administrative units. Originally under Jesuit administration until 1686, the Recollects then took over, guiding Imus toward independence from Cavite Viejo's ecclesiastical and civil control.

The Royal Order of October 30, 1776, marked the first step in Imus' separation, leading to its establishment as an independent municipality in 1795. The Recollects played a pivotal role in both religious and political emancipation.

The Imus Municipal Building, at the town center, opened in 1935. A new municipal building was inaugurated in 2003, reflecting the city's growth.

In 2009, Republic Act 9727 established Imus as Cavite's "Third District," thanks to the efforts of local congressmen and senators. Cityhood followed with Republic Act 10161, ratified by plebiscite in 2012, officially making Imus a component city.

The new City Government Center, a 30,595-square-meter facility, was completed and inaugurated in 2022, further modernizing local governance.

Today, Imus is a first-class city known for its rich history, vibrant culture, and rapid development. Landmarks like the Imus Cathedral, Heritage Park, and Battle of Alapan Monument, along with the annual Wagayway Festival, highlight its enduring legacy.
